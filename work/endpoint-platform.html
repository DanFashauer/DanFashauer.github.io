01 / Problem framing

The platform is only one part of the decision.

What appears simple

Choose a UEM platform, enroll devices, assign policy, deploy applications, and migrate users.

What actually determines success

Identity, ownership model, enrollment path, application dependencies, security controls, network access, support processes, change governance, reporting, lifecycle, and the real user workflow.

03 / Decision method

How I would structure the work.

Define the operating outcomes.Clarify who the users are, what devices they use, what business workflows matter, what security constraints exist, and what “good” support looks like.
Map technical and organizational dependencies.Identity, applications, networking, security tooling, certificates, vendors, service management, support ownership, reporting, and change control.
Separate hard requirements from preferences.This prevents tool familiarity from becoming the architecture. Requirements should drive the platform decision, not the other way around.
Design the migration and lifecycle path.Enrollment, coexistence, pilot groups, application readiness, policy parity, rollback, communications, support readiness, and steady-state ownership.
Validate the real workflow.Test user and support outcomes end-to-end, not simply whether the console reports a successful policy or enrollment.
Operationalize and measure.Document standards, establish ownership, monitor recurring failure patterns, and improve the service based on evidence.

Questions I would force into the open.

05 / Validation

What I would measure after implementation.

Technical health

Enrollment success, policy/application reliability, compliance health, deployment failures, recurring incident patterns, and lifecycle completion.

Operational health

Support effort, escalation quality, time to isolate failures, change success, user-impact signals, documentation gaps, and repeatable recovery paths.

Ownership & collaboration boundaryI bring substantial endpoint and mobility platform depth plus the systems reasoning required to understand adjacent dependencies. Identity, networking, security, cloud, application, and software specialists retain deep ownership of their domains. My role is to understand those dependencies well enough to make sound platform decisions, ask the right questions, coordinate implementation, and validate the integrated outcome.
